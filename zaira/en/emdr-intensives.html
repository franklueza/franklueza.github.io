How do I know if an EMDR intensive is for me?

It could be a good option if:

  • You have a specific experience, wound, or pattern you wish to work on.
  • What you are experiencing feels heavy, is affecting multiple areas of your life, and you don't want — or find it unfeasible — to prolong work on that topic for weeks.
  • You feel that one hour of therapy is barely enough and you seek greater continuity.
  • You have already received therapy or feel ready for more focused work.
  • Your schedule makes attending weekly therapy difficult.

It is normal to feel nervous. What matters is having sufficient stability and safety. If you are going through a crisis or if there are circumstances that could interfere with the process, you might need additional preparation or another type of support before doing an intensive.

During the evaluation, we will explore your needs and decide together if this format is right for you.

What is EMDR therapy?

EMDR (Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing) is an evidence-based therapy that helps process past experiences that have not been processed and still feel present. These experiences can keep active beliefs such as "I am not enough," "I am unsafe," or "I don't deserve...", and influence our decisions and relationships. They can also trigger automatic survival responses like fight, flight, freeze, fawn, submit, cling, or fall into codependency patterns.

Instead of leading you to remember and recount everything in detail week after week, EMDR uses bilateral stimulation — alternating stimuli from one side to the other, whether through eye movements, gentle tapping, and/or alternating sounds, whichever feels most natural to you — to help your brain do what it naturally does with material that has gotten "stuck."

What is an EMDR intensive?

An EMDR intensive is an extended, personalized therapy session. Instead of a 50-minute appointment, we set aside three to six hours to focus on a specific difficulty, experience, trigger, belief, or pattern. The exact duration will be decided together during an evaluation session and will depend on what you are working on and the pace that is right for you.

You won't spend all this time just reliving painful memories. EMDR intensives include sufficient preparation, reflection, rest breaks, and tools to help you feel present.

The word "intensive" refers to the session duration, not emotional intensity. We will have more time to work at your pace and take breaks whenever you need them.

From the comfort of your own space

All of my EMDR intensives are conducted virtually via video call. You can participate from the comfort of your home or from any private, secure space of your choice, as long as you are physically located in Texas.

No waiting rooms or driving after the session. You only need a quiet, private space and a stable internet connection. Before the intensive, we will review everything you need so you can prepare and feel at ease.

You might not call it "trauma," but it still affects you

You don't need to have experienced a single catastrophic event for your experience to be important.

Some wounds form little by little: a history of abuse, growing up amidst criticism, feeling unseen, taking on too many responsibilities, feeling you had to be the one who stayed strong, or learning to silence your needs can also leave lasting marks.

Over time, these experiences can show up as anxiety, guilt, difficulty setting boundaries, fear of rejection, relationship issues, or the feeling that what you do or have is never enough.

How Does a Virtual Intensive Work?

Every intensive is tailored to the individual and the goal they wish to work on. Generally speaking, the process includes three parts:

1. Before the Intensive

We will begin with a comprehensive 90-minute evaluation session to discuss what you wish to work on, your previous therapy experience, your support system, and determine if an intensive is the right option for you.

During this session, you may complete questionnaires or preparation exercises. This helps us use our time carefully and create a plan tailored to your needs.

Even if we decide an intensive is not the best option at this time, you will leave this session with tools and clarity regarding the best treatment for you.

2. During the Intensive

We will define a focus for the session and practice tools to help you feel connected to the present. Once sufficient preparation is in place, we can begin processing work.

The session may include conversation, reflection, bilateral stimulation (eye movements, alternating sounds, or tapping), attending to your internal parts, regulation exercises, and planned breaks.

At the end, we will set aside time to slow down, debrief what came up, and help you make the transition to wrap up the session.

3. After the Intensive

Approximately one week later, we will hold a 50-minute follow-up session to continue integrating what we processed, check in on how you've felt, reinforce anything left pending, and define your next steps.
